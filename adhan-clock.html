All Five Prayers
The 24-Hour Clock is Never Empty
Look at the clock. Every hour — from midnight to midnight — is occupied by prayer. There is no gap, no silence, no hour when the Adhan is not sounding somewhere on Earth.

157,890 mosques. Five prayers. 24 hours. The rings fill the entire clock face because the world's Muslims are distributed across every timezone — when it is Fajr in Indonesia, it is Isha in Morocco. The global community of worship never sleeps.
"Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times." — Quran 4:103
02 / 09
Dhuhr · Solar Noon
The Clearest Census
Separate the prayers and isolate the gold Dhuhr ring. Now drag the month slider from January to December. Watch carefully.

Every other ring shifts. Fajr and Isha compress and expand with the seasons. Maghrib tilts. But Dhuhr — Dhuhr barely moves.

Because Dhuhr follows solar noon, it has no seasonal distortion, no twilight complications, no high-latitude edge cases. What remains is the purest signal in the dataset: a direct portrait of where Muslims live. Every gap is an empty longitude. Every dense arc is a populated one. No other prayer gives you this cleanly.
If you wanted to draw a map of the Muslim world using only prayer data, you would use Dhuhr.
03 / 09
Fajr · Dawn
The Circle That Breathes
Separate the prayers and look at the blue Fajr ring. Now change the month slider from June to December. Watch the ring change.

In summer, Fajr disappears at high latitudes — above ~49°N, the sun never dips 18° below the horizon. The ring shows a gap where no Fajr exists astronomically. In winter, the gap closes. The ring breathes with the seasons — expanding and contracting with Earth's axial tilt.

No prayer table, no app has ever made this visible before. Here it is: the seasonal fragility of Fajr, drawn in light.
Switch to June and separate the prayers — watch for the gap in the blue ring above the 18-hour mark.
04 / 09
Maghrib · Sunset
Scholarly Consensus as Geometric Precision
When the prayers are separated, Maghrib's red ring is visibly the sharpest and densest of all five. Its edges are crisp. Its band is thin.

This is not a coincidence. Maghrib is the only prayer on which all four madhabs — Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali — agree completely. The trigger is actual sunset: when the upper limb of the sun disappears below the horizon. No interpretation. No angle. Just the sun.

The sharpest ring reflects the least disagreement. Unity produces precision.
The red ring's sharpness is a visual proof of 1,400 years of scholarly consensus.
05 / 09
Isha · Night
One Degree of Difference, Made Visible
Separate the prayers and compare the two outermost rings: Fajr (blue, innermost) and Isha (purple, outermost).

Both are twilight prayers. Both have gaps at high latitudes in summer. But look carefully — Isha's gap is slightly smaller than Fajr's.

The reason: Fajr uses 18° below the horizon. Isha uses 17°. That single degree means Isha remains calculable at slightly higher latitudes before it too disappears. One degree of solar angle, visible as arc length.

This difference — invisible in any prayer time table, invisible in any app — is only visible here.
This is why the clock exists: to reveal what numbers alone cannot show.
06 / 09
The Separation
From Chaos to Structure
Drag the centre dial from 0% to 100%. Watch what happens.

At 0%: all five prayers overlap into one dense, beautiful ring. This is the reality of global worship — inseparable, continuous, constant. The chaos is not disorder. It is the sound of the world praying.

At 100%: five distinct orbits emerge. Each prayer finds its own place. The structure of Islamic time becomes legible — five waves, each with its own character, its own story, its own geometry.

The transition between them is the moment of revelation.
"And establish prayer. Indeed, prayer restrains from immorality and wrongdoing." — Quran 29:45
07 / 09
All Five Prayers
The Gaps Are a Map
Look closely at the rings. Every prayer — Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha — has the same gaps, at the same positions on the clock. The gaps don't belong to any single prayer. They belong to the longitudes underneath them.

Those silent clock positions correspond to the Americas, the Pacific Ocean, Central Africa, and Australia — regions with few or no mosques. Where there are no Muslims, there is no Adhan.

The clock is not just a clock. It is a map of where Muslims live, expressed as time. Every gap is an empty longitude. Every dense arc is a populated one.
The clock's silences are as meaningful as its sound. Both tell you where the Muslim world is — and where it isn't.
08 / 09
Muslim World · Demographics
Thickness is Population
Separate the prayers with the dial. Now look at the circumferential thickness of each ring — how wide the band is at different clock positions. This is not decoration. It is data.

The thickest sections of every ring fall around the same hours: UTC+5 to UTC+8. That is South Asia and Southeast Asia — Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Malaysia. The most densely Muslim-populated region on Earth.

The thinner sections — Europe, West Africa, the Gulf — are real too, and visible. The Adhan Clock is a demographic portrait of the Muslim world, drawn in the language of prayer.
No census. No colour-coded map. Just 157,890 mosques, plotted by when they pray.
09 / 09
The Relay
A Prayer Never Ends
Watch the NOW marker move through the clock. Notice what it crosses.

As the marker leaves the dense S.Asia/SE Asia arc, it enters the Gulf. Then East Africa. Then West Africa and Europe. Then it returns to Asia. Each region picks up the prayer as the previous one puts it down.

This is not metaphor. It is visible in the data. The density at the NOW marker never drops to zero — there is always a mosque somewhere in its path. The Adhan is a relay race around the Earth, and no baton is ever dropped.
