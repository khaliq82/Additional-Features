How these times are calculated

Prayer times in Makkah follow the Umm al-Qura method, the official Islamic prayer calculation method of Saudi Arabia, published by Umm al-Qura University in Makkah itself. It is the method used by the Grand Mosque, Al Masjid Al Haram, and every mosque across the Kingdom.

Each of the five prayers is anchored to a precise position of the sun, observed from the coordinates of the holy city:

Fajr
Begins when the sun reaches 18.5 degrees below the eastern horizon, the first light of true dawn.
Dhuhr
Begins at Zawaal, the moment the sun passes its highest point and begins its descent westward.
Asr
Begins when the shadow of an object equals its own length, plus its shadow at noon.
Maghrib
Begins the moment the sun fully sets below the western horizon.
Isha
Begins 90 minutes after Maghrib, the fixed interval defined by the Umm al-Qura method.

The holy city

Makkah is the birthplace of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and home to the Kaaba, the qibla toward which every Muslim on Earth turns in prayer. Five times a day, the Adhan rises from the minarets of Al Masjid Al Haram and from mosques across the city, calling millions to prayer in the holiest place in Islam.

On AdhanLive, Makkah is one of the brightest points on the globe. When prayer time arrives here, you can watch its mosques light up together on the live map, part of a wave that never stops moving around the Earth.
